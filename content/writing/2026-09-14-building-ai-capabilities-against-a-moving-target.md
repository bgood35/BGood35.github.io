I agree with [OpenAI's CMO for Business, Colin Fleming, that everyone can "do Marketing"](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-happens-when-everyone-can-do-marketing-colin-fleming-b7dac?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_android&utm_campaign=share_via) because he correctly cautioned that "everyone can also create something forgettable…Someone still has to get the strategy right before the positioning, and the positioning right before the message." What should be a concern for businesses that rely on Marketing as a practice, not just marketers, is that OpenAI's [Work at the Frontier](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/work-at-the-frontier-report.pdf) showed how quickly AI has been adopted by non-marketers to execute Marketing tasks more than those related to other functions. Echoing Mark Ritson, [we need better marketers](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/two-thirds-of-american-marketers-would-fail-a-basic-marketing-test/), not more of them.

![Marketing and engineering tasks travel across many occupations](/images/marketing_and_engineering_tasks_travel_across_many_occupations.png)

I'd argue that the belief that anyone can do Marketing comes from the fact that everyone can comprehend it. But comprehension, understanding, and production are three different acts: following a signal (syntax), grounding it in the real world (semantics), and generating a new message (production). I took coding courses before building this site to improve my understanding of the skill so that I could improve the production quality of this website by giving Claude more informed direction. I do not consider myself a coder and the reason this site is not "professional grade" is because of my limited understanding. AI allows people to move directly from comprehension to production, and whether the machine can or needs to fill the void is where leading voices in the AI space disagree about the technology's potential.

I can see Suleyman's point for a closed, rule-governed system like coding. If the code is wrong, the platform doesn't execute correctly; the issue can be reverse-engineered and resolved. Marketing is not a closed system. Human behavior is [predictably irrational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predictably_Irrational), and the ability to reliably capture causal relationships to quantify its business impact remains an open debate. Where coding rewards a perfect simulation, Marketing rewards messages that are engaging, distinct, and novel; simulating 'best practices' from incomplete data about counterintuitive, emotional systems [can struggle to produce effective Marketing](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/ai-ads-are-good-enough-and-thats-problem).

![How AI-generated ads compare to human-made ads in Ipsos research](/images/ai-vs.-humans-su-ipsos-research.png)

This raises the question of who should be accountable for AI Discoverability within an organization. Given the open questions about AI's trajectory, it is a mistake for brands to invest in the creation of [bespoke divisions around the touchpoint](https://adage.com/media/media-buying-planning/aa-should-brands-advertise-to-ai-agents-faq/?utm_source=ad-age-wake-up-call&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=20260910). It is also a mistake to assign the responsibility to the SEO team because the mechanics are completely different even if they appear side-by-side in the consumer journey; SEO manages what a brand controls and AI currently favors content from third parties (reviews, creators, and press).

![Benedict Evans on the unknowns of the AI landscape](/images/benedict-evans-unknowns.png)

Influencing AI results isn't analogous to managing a channel, but to a messaging strategy focused on key opinion leaders; it should be Integrated Marketing's collective responsibility to consider AI Discoverability as a sub-segment within its plans. Making AI Discoverability a strategic exercise rather than a tactical one makes content and media decisions more deliberate, protects brands from over-investing in [tactics that can lose influence at a moment's notice](https://adage.com/influencers-creators/aa-how-brands-can-use-creators-for-ai-search-visibility/?utm_source=ad-age-wake-up-call&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=20260910) (e.g. cloaking, Reddit), and allows teams to move forward in the space without adding a line-item that needs to be funded. These are worthwhile efforts, and Marketing can seamlessly incorporate the work into its process without creating another line-item because it is responsible for producing the content that ultimately trains the LLMs. The framework below shows how Integrated Marketing can fold AI Discoverability into its existing workflow.
