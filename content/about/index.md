The fundamentals of marketing haven't changed, because the human hardware hasn't meaningfully changed in millennia. However, changes in culture, tech, and business require marketers to better understand the interconnectivity across theory, strategy, practice, technical execution, and data.

I built this site to understand firsthand how large language models consume, interpret, and prioritize content. Beyond the trainings, certifications, and opinion pieces — and the guardrails that rightly protect proprietary company data — building this site let me experience what it means to integrate A.I. into a real workflow.

My background as an audience-led, data-informed integrated marketer shapes how I see A.I. — not as a replacement for strategic thinking, but as a tool to sharpen it. My concern isn't that A.I. will replace marketers. It's that the rush to adopt it will accelerate a trend already underway: [marketing becoming less effective](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/we-ve-never-spent-more-advertising-it-s-never-done-less-us). LLMs trained on general best practices and industry averages can raise the floor of performance — but believing they generate competitive advantage is like believing you can beat the market with an index fund. Worse, marketers who treat media KPIs as proxies for business outcomes risk falling victim to [Goodhart's Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law). The answer, for me, isn't to resist A.I. but to use it more deliberately. Each month I publish a piece working through a specific question at the intersection of media strategy and A.I. This page — and everything on it — is part of that process.
