Across 15+ years in integrated marketing, I took pride in one thing: building media plans designed to drive business outcomes, not just strong media metrics. Studying advertising in college and starting my career at leading media agencies taught me the craft of communications planning and media strategy. Going brand-side taught me something the agency seat can't show you: how investment decisions actually get made, when dollars are contested from multiple divisions at all times.

That crossover sharpened a problem I'd been circling for years: Even as marketing has grown more technical and measurable, [it has arguably become less effective at growing businesses.](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/we-ve-never-spent-more-advertising-it-s-never-done-less-us) Brands understandably want recommendations that show a strong ROI to justify the spend internally, but too often that number becomes the real deliverable. I consider myself an audience-led, data-informed marketer, but those who treat media KPIs as proxies for business outcomes risk falling victim to [Goodhart's Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law). That's the problem I want this site to address.

My goal is to connect two things that usually get separated: how you measure what an endeavor is worth to the business, and the interconnectivity between theory, strategy, practice, technical execution, and data that both shapes that number and is shaped by it.

It's worth noting that this website itself is an example of this type of discussion. I started this project as something smaller: an experiment in building a "digital twin," which meant a few coding classes, a Claude subscription, and putting a real point of view into an AI-friendly format. It became the place where I rediscovered how much I enjoy working on a problem in the open and how writing forces me to more strongly defend my reasoning. You can say that while the initiative as originally scoped did not meet its objective, the unintended consequences produced significant ROI that I had never thought to account for.
