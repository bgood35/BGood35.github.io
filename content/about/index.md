This website is an experiment.

I built it using Hugo and GitHub Pages, learning the technical stack from scratch with A.I. assistance — not because I needed a website, but because I wanted to understand how large language models consume, interpret, and prioritize content. That curiosity started with an AEO/GEO roadmap I was developing for my brands and has since expanded into a broader question: what does it actually mean to work effectively with A.I. in a marketing context?

I've spent my career in media strategy and communication planning, working at the intersection of consumer behavior, channel dynamics, and business outcomes. That background shapes how I think about A.I. — not as a replacement for strategic thinking, but as a reason to sharpen it.

My concern is not that A.I. will replace marketers. It's that the rush to adopt it will accelerate a trend that was already underway: [marketing becoming less effective](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/we-ve-never-spent-more-advertising-it-s-never-done-less-us). LLMs trained on general best practices and industry averages can raise the floor of performance — but the belief that they can generate competitive advantage is akin to believing you can beat the stock market by investing in index funds. Worse, marketers who treat media KPIs as reliable proxies for business outcomes risk falling victim to [Goodhart's Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law).

The answer, for me, is not to resist A.I. but to use it more deliberately. Each month I publish a piece working through a specific question at the intersection of media strategy and A.I. This page — and everything on it — is part of that process.
